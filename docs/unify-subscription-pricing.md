# Unify's own subscription — how a tenant admin pays for the platform

Written for: engineers working on this codebase, and whoever is deciding what
Unify charges.

Before this existed, any business that registered got the entire platform for
nothing. This is the layer that makes Unify a product a tenant admin *buys*:
a price list, a way to pay for it, a gate that decides what their plan allows,
and the owner-side console that shows what it all earns.

The one distinction to hold on to, because two things in this codebase are
called "subscription":

| | What it is | Code |
|---|---|---|
| **Unify subscription** | What a **tenant pays Unify** for the platform | `Models/PlatformBilling/*`, `Services/PlatformBilling/*`, `/api/subscription` |
| **Membership** | What a **tenant's own customers pay the tenant** (gym memberships and the like) | `Models/Subscription.cs`, `/api/subscriptions` |

They never share a table, a gateway credential or a screen. Mixing the two
would route a subscription payment into a tenant's own Stripe account, which
is the worst bug this feature could have.

## The pricing strategy, and why it is Tinder's

The brief was to use Tinder's marketing and pricing playbook. Tinder is the
best-documented consumer subscription funnel there is, and almost every piece
of it has a reason that survives the translation to SME software. Each row
below is a thing Tinder does, what it becomes here, and where it lives.

| Tinder | Unify | Where |
|---|---|---|
| A free tier that genuinely works, capped not crippled | **Starter**: 1 branch, 2 team members, 60 bookings/month, invoices, receipts, email reminders, a public directory listing. Free forever, no card. | `PlatformPlanCatalog` |
| Three paid rungs — Plus / Gold / Platinum | **Grow / Pro / Prime**. Prime's main job is to make Pro look reasonable. | `PlatformPlanCatalog` |
| The middle rung sells *information* ("See Who Likes You"), not volume | **Pro** sells the AI copilots and demand analytics — things an owner cannot work out by being patient on the free tier. It sits above the "unlimited bookings" rung deliberately. | `PlatformPlanCatalog` |
| "Most popular" badge on the middle rung | `IsMostPopular` on Pro, and exactly one plan may carry it (there is a test) | `PlanCards.tsx` |
| A steep 1 / 6 / 12-month ladder, monthly as the anchor | 30% off at 6 months, 45% at 12. Tinder's go past 60%; a B2B renewal cannot bear that. | `PlatformPlanCatalog.Terms` |
| Consumables sold in packs, bigger pack = better unit price | **Spotlight** (24h at the top of the directory — Tinder's Boost), **AI credits** (Super Like), **message credits**, **extra seats** | `PlatformAddOnCatalog` |
| Boosts sold to free users too | Spotlight has `MinimumTier = 0`. A Starter tenant paying LKR 1,900 for a busy weekend is the first money they ever give us, which is the hard part. | `PlatformAddOnCatalog` |
| A free Boost a month for subscribers, to teach the habit | `SpotlightsPerMonth` on every paid plan, granted at the start of each term and expiring with it | `PlanLimits`, `GrantTermCreditsAsync` |
| A cheap first term | `FIRST50` — 50% off the first term, auto-applied to a business that has never paid. The renewal is at list price and the quote says so. | `PlatformPromotionSeeder` |
| Paywall at the moment of need | Every gate answers **402** with the exact limit hit and the cheapest plan that clears it. One interceptor, one sheet, over whatever the user was doing. | `RequiresPlanAttribute`, `PaywallProvider.tsx` |
| Auto-renew by default | `AutoRenew` on, cancellation takes effect at the end of the term | `PlatformSubscription` |
| Regional pricing | LKR for the home market, USD for everyone else, both from the same catalogue | `PlatformPlanCatalog.Currencies` |

### The one thing deliberately not copied

Tinder also varied its price by the **buyer's age**. That produced a class
action in California and an ACCC finding in Australia, and it is the part of
the playbook that is a liability rather than a tactic.

The personalised half is reproduced here as offers earned by **behaviour**,
which a business chooses, rather than by demographics, which a person does
not:

- `FIRST50` — a business that has never held a paid plan
- `COMEBACK40` — a business whose paid plan has lapsed
- `ANNUAL20` — a term-restricted public code

No demographic value is an input to any price this system quotes. See the
comment on `Models/PlatformBilling/PlatformPromotion.cs`.

## What is gated, and how

`PlanLimits` (stored as a plan's `LimitsJson`) holds three kinds of thing:

- **Feature flags** — `PlanFeatures.AiAgents`, `InventoryPro`, … An endpoint
  asks for one with `[RequiresPlanFeature(...)]`.
- **Caps that count live rows** — branches, seats, resources. Checked at the
  call site with `IEntitlementService.CheckCapAsync`, because the count that
  matters is the current one; a closed branch gives its slot back.
- **Monthly meters** — bookings, AI runs, SMS. Applied with
  `[MetersPlanQuota(...)]`, counted *after* the action and only on a success,
  so a copilot call that timed out has not spent anybody's allowance.

A cap is a count of records; a meter is a count of events. A booking that was
later cancelled still used the capacity, which is why the booking meter counts
creations rather than rows — otherwise a tenant could delete their way back
under the cap.

Where the gates are applied today:

| Gate | Endpoint |
|---|---|
| `InventoryPro` | `PurchaseOrdersController`, `SuppliersController` |
| `AdvancedBilling` | `InsuranceClaimsController`, `CommissionRulesController` |
| `CustomBranding` | `DynamicFormsController`, `InvoiceTemplatesController` |
| `PaymentGateways` | `PaymentGatewaysController` |
| `AiAgents` + AI-run meter | `AgentWorkflowController`, `BillingAgentController`, `WorkspaceAssistantController` |
| Branch cap | `BranchesController.Create` |
| Seat cap | `UsersController.CreateUser` (customers are never seats) |
| Booking meter | `BookingsController.Create` |

### The 402 contract

```jsonc
{
  "status": "upgradeRequired",
  "message": "You have used all 60 bookings in your plan this month.",
  "paywall": {
    "reason": "quota",          // feature | cap | quota
    "feature": null,
    "metric": "bookings",
    "used": 60,
    "limit": 60,
    "currentPlan": "starter",
    "requiredPlan": "grow",
    "requiredPlanName": "Grow",
    "addOnCode": null,          // a pack that would also clear it
    "upgradeUrl": "/subscription"
  }
}
```

402 is used nowhere else in this API, so a client can key on the status code
alone. The React app has one interceptor (`PaywallProvider`) and the Flutter
app one reader (`PlanPaywall.of`).

## Paying

Unify's own merchant accounts come from **configuration**, never the database,
so no tenant-facing code path can read or rewrite them:

```
Platform:Billing:Currency               LKR
Platform:Billing:TestMode               true unless explicitly "false"
Platform:Billing:Stripe:SecretKey       sk_…
Platform:Billing:Stripe:PublicKey       pk_…
Platform:Billing:Stripe:WebhookSecret   whsec_…
Platform:Billing:PayPal:ClientId
Platform:Billing:PayPal:ClientSecret
Platform:Billing:PayPal:WebhookId
Platform:Billing:SettlementCurrency     e.g. USD — Stripe will not take LKR
Platform:Billing:Rates:LKR              units of LKR per 1 settlement unit
```

The `IPaymentProcessor` implementations (Stripe, PayPal, Manual) are shared
with the tenant-facing billing engine — the integrations are written once.
Only the credentials differ.

**With nothing configured the sandbox provider runs**, exactly as the
tenant-facing engine does: the whole flow works end to end and says plainly
that it was simulated, rather than pretending a card was charged.

Webhooks: `POST /api/subscription/webhooks/{provider}` — anonymous, with the
provider's signature as the authentication, and no tenant in the URL because
the payment row is what says whose money it was.

### The rule the flow exists to enforce

A plan is granted by `ActivateAsync`, and `ActivateAsync` is only ever reached
from a payment the **gateway** says succeeded — a webhook, or a status read
back from the provider. The browser can ask us to check; it can never assert.

## Lifecycle

```
                    ┌──────────┐
     trial ────────▶│ Trialing │──── 14 days ────▶ Starter
                    └────┬─────┘
                         │ pays
                         ▼
  Starter ── pays ──▶ ┌────────┐ ── cancels ──▶ ┌────────────┐
   (free)             │ Active │                │ Cancelling │
       ▲              └───┬────┘                └──────┬─────┘
       │                  │ renewal unpaid             │ term ends
       │                  ▼                            │
       │            ┌──────────┐  14-day grace         │
       └────────────│ PastDue  │───────────────────────┘
          grace     └──────────┘
          lapses
```

Three things are true throughout, and they are the whole retention argument:

1. **Nothing is ever deleted.** A business that stops paying loses the paid
   features and keeps every row of its data. The cheapest customer to win back
   is the one who never had to leave.
2. **A failed renewal does not lock the account.** It opens a 14-day grace
   window where everything keeps working and the tenant gets chased.
3. **Cancelling keeps every day that was paid for.** Only a trial can be
   cancelled immediately, because no money moved.

Unify does not vault cards, so a renewal raises an invoice and asks the tenant
to pay it rather than charging off-session. The grace window is what makes
that a workable flow instead of an outage.

`PlatformRenewalService` sweeps hourly: trials that ended, renewal bills three
days ahead of the term, past-due transitions, and the drop back to Starter.

## Credits

The ledger is **lots**, not a running total. Every grant is a row with its own
expiry and its own `Remaining`, and a spend draws down the lot that expires
soonest.

The alternative — one balance filtered by expiry at read time — cannot say
which grant a spend came out of, so a tenant who spends from a bought pack and
then watches a free monthly grant lapse loses credits they actually paid for.
One extra column makes the balance arithmetic instead of a guess.

Balance = the sum of `Remaining` over grant rows that have not expired. The
negative rows are the audit trail of spends; they are never part of the
balance.

## Screens

**Tenant admin**

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| `/pricing` (web, public) | The price list, term toggle with savings, comparison table, FAQ. Reads the same endpoint checkout prices against, so it cannot drift. |
| `/subscription` (web, Admin + Manager) | Current plan, usage meters, plan ladder, add-on store, invoices, cancel/resume |
| Business → *Your Unify Plan* (Flutter) | The same, on the phone |

Managers can see the plan and why a screen is locked; only an **Admin** can
commit the business to a bill.

**Platform owner** — `/platform/revenue`, behind the same MFA-backed policy as
the rest of the console: MRR, ARR, ARPA, collected, free→paid conversion,
trial conversion, churn, plan and term mix, every subscription, every invoice.
Two levers, both requiring a fresh authenticator code and both written to the
audit log:

- **Comp** — a plan free for 12 months (a pilot, a partner, an apology).
  Excluded from MRR, so comping an account cannot quietly inflate the figures.
- **Extend** — push a term out 30 days at no charge and clear a past-due state.

Every rate on that page is `null` rather than zero when there is nothing to
divide by. A platform in its first week showing "0% trial conversion" reads as
a business that is failing, when in truth nothing has happened yet — so it
renders as a dash, the same rule the clinic and gym dashboards follow.

## Schema

Migration `20260929113230_AddPlatformSubscriptionBilling`, 11 tables:
`platform_plans`, `platform_plan_prices`, `platform_subscriptions`,
`platform_subscription_events`, `platform_invoices`, `platform_payments`,
`platform_addons`, `platform_credit_entries`, `platform_usage_counters`,
`platform_promotions`, `platform_promotion_redemptions`.

None of them carry a tenant query filter, for the same reason the platform
console tables do not: the renewal worker, the gateway webhook and the owner's
revenue screen all read them with no tenant in context. Every tenant-facing
query filters by `TenantId` by hand, from the token.

A tenant created before this feature existed gets its free Starter row the
first time anything asks (`EnsureSubscriptionAsync`), so there is no backfill
to run.

## Tests

`backend/SmeBackend.Tests/PlatformBilling/` — 55 tests over three files:

- `PlatformPricingTests` — the ladder is ordered, each tier is a superset of
  the one below, term savings are exactly 30/45%, longer terms always cost
  less per month, no savings badge overstates its discount, the bigger pack is
  always better value, syncing twice is a no-op.
- `EntitlementTests` — feature gates, caps, meters, credit-lot FIFO and
  expiry, and what a past-due or lapsed subscription still grants.
- `SubscriptionLifecycleTests` — quoting, proration, trials, upgrade and
  downgrade events, cancel/resume, dropping to Starter, add-ons, offers.
